01 — Research

On the design of an inlet cowl lip of a hypersonic scramjet

Undergraduate honors thesis, Rodriguez Flow Group. In progress.

Advisor
Prof. Mauro Rodriguez
Reader
Prof. Kenny Breuer
Years
2026–2027
Tools
Python, SCRAM (2D Navier–Stokes), MFC on Oscar HPC

A scramjet compresses incoming air using shocks instead of moving parts. When the shock from the cowl lip hits the boundary layer on the opposite wall, it can lift the flow off the wall into a recirculation zone. That bubble blocks part of the inlet and heats the wall sharply where the flow reattaches. If it grows large enough, the inlet unstarts and the engine loses thrust.

The thesis asks where the boundary between attached and separated flow sits as a function of Mach number and deflection angle. I reduced the problem with dimensional analysis to four varied groups (M∞, α, Re, H/Lc), built an inviscid oblique-shock model in Python as the baseline, and am now running 2D laminar Navier–Stokes simulations with heat conduction to map the separated regime, comparing adiabatic and cold isothermal walls.

Early runs used the MFC solver on Brown's Oscar cluster. I moved to SCRAM once it was clear the viscous physics needed temperature-dependent viscosity, a true wall boundary condition and a wall-refined grid.

Wind Tunnel Airfoil Analysis

Murphy Paul · ENGN 1700 · Brown University · Spring 2026

Overview

Measured lift and drag on a finite-span NACA wing in a subsonic wind tunnel using a two-channel strain-gauge balance. Calibrated raw voltage signals to moments, converted them to normal and axial forces, then resolved those into lift and drag as a function of angle of attack. Results were compared against XFLR5 panel-method simulations and finite-aspect-ratio (FAR) lifting-line theory.

Supersonic Nozzle Optimization

Murphy Paul, Charlie Case, Carter Smith · The Area Ratio Rebels · Brown University · Spring 2026

Overview

Designed and optimized a converging-diverging exhaust nozzle for a Mach 1.7 civil transport (Boom Overture class) cruising at 60,000 ft. Used quasi-1D isentropic nozzle theory to sweep a 1,600-point parameter space, balancing two competing objectives: maximizing cruise thrust coefficient CF and limiting takeoff jet velocity to meet FAA noise limits.

Recommended design

Witoszynski profile at Ae/A* ≈ 2.74, L/rt ≈ 3.2. It removes both divergence loss (zero exit slope) and the exit non-uniformity penalty (zero throat curvature, κt = 0), and its shorter optimal length keeps friction low. It gave the highest net cruise CF of the profiles tested, with takeoff noise and shock feasibility constraints satisfied.

Wall formula

Witoszynski diverging section: r(ξ) = r_t + (r_e − r_t) · f(ξ) f(ξ) = 6ξ⁵ − 15ξ⁴ + 10ξ³ ξ ∈ [0, 1] f′(0) = f′(1) = 0 · f″(0) = f″(1) = 0

Unexpected finding

A larger Ae/A* places a stronger normal shock deeper in the diverging section at takeoff, but the isentropic expansion after it to a larger exit area reduces exit velocity compared with a smaller nozzle. So the noise and efficiency objectives pointed to the same area ratio of about 2.74, removing what looked like a fundamental design conflict.

Hypersonic Scramjet Inlet: Technical Notes

Murphy Paul · Honors Thesis · Rodriguez Flow Group, Brown University · 2026–2027

Problem

A uniform M∞ stream flows over a flat no-slip wall where a laminar boundary layer develops. A thin plate at angle α (the cowl lip) sits a height H above the wall, a distance Lc downstream of the wall's leading edge. Its oblique shock crosses the channel and strikes the wall boundary layer. If the shock is strong enough, the boundary layer separates. The main quantity of interest is the recirculation length Lsep = xreat − xsep, where the wall skin friction changes sign. Secondary quantities are the reflected-shock location, upstream influence, and peak wall pressure and heat flux.

Solver

The first viscous runs used MFC on Brown's Oscar cluster and resolved the inviscid shock structure of a Mach 6 compression ramp. Constant viscosity, an immersed-boundary wall with an unverified thermal condition, and roughly five cells across the boundary layer ruled it out for the viscous study. Current runs use SCRAM, a 2D compressible Navier–Stokes solver in Python with Fourier heat conduction, temperature-dependent viscosity, an adiabatic or isothermal wall at the domain boundary, and a wall-refined grid.

Status

Grid convergence is the main open item: a 160×64 / 320×128 / 640×256 refinement series with Richardson extrapolation is running. After that comes the Mach 6 campaign and the separation regime map.
